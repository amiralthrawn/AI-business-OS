# Step 12 — Dataset design and justification

## Why this volume of data

The dataset is intentionally small in row count (1 company, 3 suppliers, 5 products,
3 customers, ~30 transactions) but structured with real temporal depth (~6 months,
6 data points per series) rather than a single snapshot. The goal was never "more
rows" — it was **enough points per series to make a baseline-vs-recent comparison
meaningful**. `app/core/analytics.py` splits a chronologically-ordered series in
half and compares the two halves; four points is the minimum for that split to
produce two non-trivial groups, and six points (three per half) is enough to look
like a real trend rather than a coin flip. Millions of synthetic rows would not
have demonstrated anything additional about the architecture and would have made
the seed slower and the demo data harder to reason about by hand.

## Relations exercised

Every relation the Data Core actually supports is exercised at least once:
`Supplier → Product → Transaction`, `Customer → Transaction`, `Transaction →
Product`, and `Document`/`Communication` linked polymorphically to a Supplier and
a Customer respectively. No new entities were introduced; the only schema change
was one nullable column, `Transaction.expected_at`, added specifically so
supplier delivery performance could be computed from real promise-vs-actual
dates instead of a hardcoded proxy.

## Causal chains present

The dataset encodes five distinct, independently detectable signals, chosen to
cover Procurement, Finance, and Sales/CRM without needing new business domains:

1. **Reactive supplier cost spike** (Pacific Components / Sensor Module) — a
   stable 3-month cost history, then a live 25% increase triggered through the
   real `ProcurementService` at seed time (not inserted as a finished Risk) →
   `SupplierCostIncreased` → `RiskCreated` → `TaskCreated`. This is the exact
   pipeline built in steps 5–7, re-verified by the richer dataset.
2. **Gradual margin deterioration** (Northline Steel / Steel Frame Assembly) —
   unit cost creeps up ~17% over 6 months while three customers' combined
   buying pattern leaves aggregate revenue roughly flat, so the margin computed
   by `compute_margin_trend` compresses about 11 points, crossing the
   "deteriorating" threshold.
3. **Supplier delivery performance decline** (Iberia Logistics Parts /
   Hydraulic Hose 2m) — cost stays flat; only the promise-to-delivery gap grows
   from ~1 day to ~5 days, isolating a delivery-specific signal from a
   cost-specific one.
4. **Customer decline / churn risk** (BrightWorks Ltd) — order volume for Steel
   Frame Assembly drops roughly 60% between the first and second half of the
   period.
5. **Customer growth opportunity** (Metroline Corp) — the same product, the
   opposite trend: order volume roughly doubles.

A sixth customer, **Vantix Group**, buys the same product at a near-constant
rate throughout — the deliberate "nothing happening here" control case, proving the
system's rules produce silence on routine data rather than an alert for every
row (see `test_stable_entities_trigger_no_risk_or_opportunity`).

## Demonstrable scenarios

- `python -m data.seed` reproduces this exact state deterministically and
  prints how many Risks/Opportunities were detected.
- `POST /ai/ask` with "Why is our margin declining on the Steel Frame
  Assembly?" pulls Finance + Procurement + Sales capabilities in one request.
- `POST /ai/ask` with "What deserves my attention today?" summarizes every
  open Risk/Opportunity.
- `GET /home` surfaces all five signals as ranked "AI Priorities", each linking
  to a detail page built from the same data.
- `POST /intelligence/monitor`, run twice, detects the same signals once and
  reports zero on the second call (idempotence).

## What is simulated

- **All business data** is synthetic, generated by `data/seed.py` — no real
  customers, suppliers, invoices or communications exist behind this.
- **External Intelligence** (market prices, competitor data, economic
  indicators) is entirely absent, not even a placeholder table. Nothing in
  this step depends on it — this is the honest state, not a partial mock.
- **HR and Marketing** have no data model at all (see `brain/decisions.md` for
  why this was scoped out of step 12).
- The **monitoring sweep** is a manual trigger (`POST /intelligence/monitor`),
  standing in for what would be a scheduled job in production.

## What real production data would require

- Real transactional feeds (ERP/accounting exports or APIs) for Finance and
  Procurement, not manual seed rows.
- A real delivery/logistics feed to populate `expected_at` reliably — today it
  only exists in synthetic data.
- CRM/Sales system integration for customer and pipeline data.
- A proper data-quality layer: production data will have gaps, duplicates and
  inconsistent units that this seed, by construction, does not have.

## MVP limits carried forward

- Only Finance, Procurement and Sales/CRM are modeled with real analytical
  depth. HR, Marketing and Supply Chain remain structural placeholders.
- Trend detection is a simple first-half-vs-second-half split, not a proper
  time series model (no seasonality, no outlier handling).
- The dataset has exactly one instance of each signal type; the rules have not
  been tested against noisier, more ambiguous real-world data.
